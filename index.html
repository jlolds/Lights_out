Lights Out in Bethesda

A War Game: The Government Shuts Down and NIH Goes Dark

Scenario

OCTOBER 1, 12:01 AM. Congress failed to pass a budget. The federal government is shut down. At NIH in Bethesda, thousands of scientists, nurses, and staff are told to go home. But the hospital still has patients, the lab mice still need to eat, and the freezers still need to stay cold.

The Rules

  • During a shutdown, federal work may continue only if it protects human life or government property.
  • Everyone else goes home, unpaid.
  • Working for free is illegal. No volunteering.
  • Patients already in the NIH hospital (the Clinical Center) keep getting care. New patients are turned away.

Your instructor plays everyone else: the White House, the HHS Secretary, the lawyers, and the Senate. The instructor decides what happens as a result of your choices.

Fictional scenario, loosely based on real shutdowns.

Teams

Three breakout rooms. Pick a role, a Spokesperson, and a Scribe. Extra people are advisors.

Room A: NIH Leadership

You decide who keeps working. You have 3 "Keep Open" cards.

  • NIH Director
  • Hospital (Clinical Center) Chief
  • Chief Lawyer: makes sure every card follows the life-or-property rule
  • Press Secretary

Room B: The Scientists

You were just sent home. You have no cards, only arguments.

  • Dr. Alvarez: 3-year mouse study, now in Year 2
  • Dr. Okafor: freezers holding 15 years of patient samples
  • Dr. Hollis: runs a children's cancer trial with a waiting list
  • Jordan: a postdoc who just got a job offer from a biotech company

Room C: Congress, Press & Families

You can't run NIH, but you control the money and the story.

  • Rep. Whitfield: represents Bethesda
  • Sen. Calder: the other party, wants to win the shutdown fight
  • A TV reporter
  • The Kowalskis: parents of Ellie, age 9, who has a rare cancer and was about to enter an NIH trial

Move 1: Day 1, "Who Stays?"

OCTOBER 1, 7:00 AM. Six requests land on the NIH Director's desk. There are only three cards.
#Request to Keep Open
1Admit new patients to trials (like Ellie)
2Feed and care for lab animals
3Watch the freezers of patient samples
4Keep the 3-year mouse study running
5Keep the outbreak lab tracking a new virus
6Keep the press office open

Room A: NIH Leadership

  1. Pick 3 requests to keep open. For each, the lawyer writes one sentence: life or property?
  2. Dr. Alvarez says she'll sneak in Saturday, unpaid, to check her mice. Do you stop her?

Room B: The Scientists

  1. Pick the one request you'll fight hardest for. Write your best one-sentence pitch.
  2. Jordan's job offer: tell Jordan to stay or go?

Room C: Congress, Press & Families

  1. Congress: reopen everything, reopen only NIH, or hold out for your side's demands?
  2. Reporter: write tonight's headline (10 words or fewer).
REPORT OUT: Each Spokesperson gets 1 minute. The instructor then tells you what happened to everything left closed.

Move 2: Day 12, "It Gets Personal"

OCTOBER 12. No end in sight. Ellie Kowalski is on the morning news. Her parents say NIH "shut the door on our daughter." The clip has 4 million views. The HHS Secretary tells NIH: "No interviews. No comment."

Room A: NIH Leadership

  1. Do you use one of your 3 cards to admit Ellie? (Dozens of other families on the waiting list aren't on TV.) If yes, what do you close instead?
  2. The Director is invited on a Sunday news show. Obey the Secretary, or go on?

Room B: The Scientists

  1. Go public with an open letter to Congress, or stay quiet? (You're federal employees.)
  2. When the lights come back on, what's the first thing NIH should restart?

Room C: Congress, Press & Families

  1. An advocacy group wants Ellie at a press conference at the Capitol. She is 9. Do the Kowalskis bring her?
  2. Congress: has Ellie changed your position?
  3. Reporter: final headline.
REPORT OUT: 1 minute per team. Then the instructor announces when the shutdown ends.

Debrief

Class vote: Best headline?
  • A freezer counts as "property." A 9-year-old who hasn't been admitted yet may not count as "life." Is that the right rule?
  • Why would the law forbid people from working for free?
  • Did putting Ellie on TV help her? Did it help the families who weren't on TV?
  • What does a shutdown cost science that never shows up in the budget?
